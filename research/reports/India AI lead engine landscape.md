# Turn India's company births into welcomed conversations

The product that wins this market is not a cheaper Indian copy of an AI SDR. It is a **consent-native permission engine for company births**, and SolutionAI is unusually well placed to build it. Globally, value is draining out of static contact databases, where ZoomInfo's Q2 2026 revenue grew 1.2% with 89% net revenue retention, and into orchestration and agents, where Clay raised at **$7.1B** in September 2026. At the same time, the first wave of autonomous AI SDRs has shown that high-volume outreach without permission burns sending domains, brands and customers. None of the global tools can see an Indian company at birth, because they key on domains, LinkedIn URLs and work emails that a week-old private limited company does not have, and none of them offers Hindi, the WhatsApp Business API or TRAI-compliant calling. Inside India the incumbent playbook is the reverse of world-class: daily lists of new companies with director mobile numbers are sold openly, banks and compliance firms phone founders within 24 hours of incorporation, and regulation is now closing that route. TRAI's 2025 rules can disconnect an unregistered telemarketer for a year, WhatsApp requires an opt-in that names the business, and the DPDP Act's consent duties start on **13 May 2027** with no legitimate-interests ground. SolutionAI should therefore build a living graph of every new Indian entity keyed on its CIN or LLPIN (extending the UP Startup Atlas nationally), infer needs from statutory deadlines and honest scheme eligibility rather than guessed intent, let a restraint engine decide whether to contact anyone at all, make the first touch a verifiable piece of value that captures consent, use WhatsApp and Hindi/English voice only after that consent, and close the loop through its verified CA/CS marketplace so that attribution runs to a paid service and an on-time filing. Voice, WhatsApp and crawling are commodities to rent; the moat is the registry-native graph, the consent ledger and the outcome loop. Two caveats apply throughout: social-media voice-of-customer evidence is thin, because Reddit was blocked and the shared search budget ran out before LinkedIn, X, G2 or YouTube could be mined, and most performance figures in this industry are vendor-reported and are labelled as such below.

## Value has moved from owning contact data to orchestrating it

Global data and enrichment platforms build their records in three ways, and the economics of the three now diverge sharply. **Incumbents** own databases fed by contributory networks that harvest email-signature blocks from their own users. ZoomInfo claims 500M contacts, 100M companies and more than 300 human researchers ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/what-is-zoominfo)), and says its network verifies about **20M email signatures a month** ([ZoomInfo FAQ](https://atlas.zoominfo.com/faqs/data/how-does-zoominfo-get-my-info)); Apollo describes more than 2M data contributors, web crawlers and over 200M licensed records processed monthly ([Apollo](https://knowledge.apollo.io/hc/en-us/articles/19331318468621)); Lusha runs a similar community programme and fills gaps with pattern-based email completion ([Lusha](https://www.lusha.com/data-sources/)). **Orchestrators** own little data. Clay chains **150+ providers** into per-field waterfalls that stop at the first validated hit and charge nothing when a lookup comes back empty ([Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)), and fills long-tail fields with Claygent, a web-research agent that returns a reasoning trace for every decision and has run more than 5 billion times ([Claygent](https://www.clay.com/claygent)). **Signal platforms** such as 6sense, Demandbase, Common Room, Warmly and Unify concentrate on resolving pixels, IP addresses, CRM rows and third-party intent (usually Bombora's publisher co-op) onto one canonical account or person record ([Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/how-demandbase-aims-redefine-gtm-connected-ai-powered-agents); [Warmly](https://www.warmly.ai/p/pricing)). No vendor publishes its entity-resolution method. The only public design principle is People Data Labs' emphasis on preventing false linkages, which treats wrongly merging two entities as the worse error ([PDL docs](https://docs.peopledatalabs.com/docs/data-sources)).

Money is following orchestration. Clay raised **$115M at $7.1B** on 9 September 2026 with 17k+ customers and revenue that grew 4x in 2025 ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/blog/series-d)); Sacra estimates its run-rate at about **$200M** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/clay/)). In March 2026 Clay cut marketplace data prices by 50–90% while metering platform "Actions" separately, an explicit bet that data margins will shrink and workflow margins will hold ([Metronome](https://metronome.com/blog/pricing-model-spotlight-why-clay-cut-data-prices-by-50-to-win-the-long-game)). ZoomInfo, the largest database, reported Q2 2026 revenue of $310.4M (**+1.2%**), net revenue retention of **89%**, downmarket ACV down 12% and a $650.5M goodwill impairment ([ZoomInfo Q2 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260805496533/en/)), and has moved to consumption pricing with a free entry point. Apollo passed $145M ARR in May 2025 ([Apollo](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/apolloios-ai-platform-is-rewriting-the-sales-playbook-with-500-percent-growth-in-12-months-302454754.html)) and was reported to be approaching $200M alongside a CEO change in February 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/enterprise-software-deals/2026/02/03/sales-engagement-apollo-io-ceo-change)). Consolidation is under way: Zoom bought Common Room in July 2026 ([Zoom](https://www.zoom.com/en/blog/zoom-to-acquire-common-room/)), and ZoomInfo built 50+ prebuilt agent teams on its DoubleO.ai acquisition ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/what-is-zoominfo)). Pricing has split into low-entry credit SaaS (Clay $185/month, Apollo $49–119 per user per month, Lusha $49.90–399.90/month, PDL $98/month) and annual enterprise contracts. Common Room's entry tier is **$2,500/month billed annually** ([Common Room](https://www.commonroom.io/pricing/)), Warmly costs $10–30k a year ([Warmly](https://www.warmly.ai/p/pricing)), and ZoomInfo's median contract is about $31.9k a year according to Vendr data cited by a competitor ([Gojiberry](https://blog.gojiberry.ai/blog/zoominfo-pricing)). The signal tier sits an order of magnitude above what an Indian MSME can pay.

Users reward convenience and coverage and punish inaccuracy and opaque billing. Apollo holds **4.7/5 across 9,500+ G2 reviews** for its all-in-one database, sequencer and dialer. Yet a competitor's review reports **15–25% bounce rates** on large exported lists, mid-month credit rationing, and accuracy of 88% in the US against 60–73% elsewhere ([MailReach, competitor](https://www.mailreach.co/blog/apollo-io-review)). Seamless.AI's Trustpilot reviewers describe 60-day written-notice and auto-renewal traps ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/seamless.ai?page=1)), and Clay's 2026 repricing drew complaints that the same workflow is now billed twice, once as Actions and once as Data Credits ([Bitscale, competitor](https://bitscale.ai/blogs/clay-pricing-changes-15k-action-limits-higher-costs-and-agency-risk)). The gap between advertised 95–98% accuracy and practitioners' bounce rates is the category's core trust problem. It rewards three cheap behaviours: verify at send time, show confidence and freshness for each field, and charge only for verified results. Even this evidence is second-hand, because primary Reddit, Hacker News and X threads on data vendors could not be retrieved.

For India, the decisive fact is a blind spot. No data-vendor page reviewed mentions Indian coverage. Cognism markets European B2B data ([Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/pricing)), Unify's roadmap lists expanded EMEA/APAC coverage as a future item ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/unify/)), HubSpot keys company records on the **domain** ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/get-started-with-data-enrichment)), PDL takes job postings only from company career pages ([PDL](https://docs.peopledatalabs.com/docs/data-sources)), and ZoomInfo's regulatory sources are SEC filings. A company incorporated last week has a CIN, a registered address and director names, but usually no domain, website, LinkedIn page or email-signature trail. Global tools therefore cannot discover it or enrich it until it builds a footprint, and bootstrapped MSMEs that never raise money or hire may never trigger their new-company signals at all. That is an inference from the vendors' stated keys rather than a vendor admission, but it holds across every product reviewed. Harmonic, the startup database for VCs, tracks 30M+ companies through hiring, funding and stealth-founder signals ([Harmonic](https://www.harmonic.ai)) and shows the same pattern: the global startup source of truth is a web-footprint graph. These platforms remain useful later, as waterfall steps once a domain exists, but they are not a discovery layer for Indian company births.

## AI SDRs proved that volume without permission destroys itself

Almost every outreach product now runs the same pipeline: pick a list, enrich it, research each lead with an LLM, write per-contact copy, send from rotated mailboxes, classify replies and book meetings. The real differences lie in who owns the sending reputation, where data and signals come from, how much autonomy is the default, and whether the motion is cold outbound or fast response to inbound leads. The most profitable players sell infrastructure, not intelligence. **lemlist** reportedly reached **$53M ARR in May 2026** without outside capital, and **Instantly** reached $20M by March 2024 ([GetLatka lemlist](https://getlatka.com/companies/lemlist); [GetLatka Instantly](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly.ai)); both sell unlimited mailboxes, warm-up, rotation and placement testing for $39–379 a month ([Smartlead pricing](https://www.smartlead.ai/pricing); [Instantly pricing](https://instantly.ai/pricing)). Enterprise sales-engagement platforms price opaquely around seats plus AI credits, with a Vendr median of $45,600 a year for Outreach ([Claap](https://www.claap.io/blog/clari-competitors)). Autonomous agents price like headcount: 11x's Alice lists at **$3,750 a month billed annually** ([Coldreach](https://coldreach.ai/blog/11x.ai-review)). The newest units are tied to outcomes. HubSpot's Prospecting Agent costs **$1 per lead** it recommends for outreach ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/ai-prospecting-agent)), AiSDR charges **$1.50 per person prospected** ([AiSDR](https://aisdr.com/pricing/)), and Agentforce charges $0.10 per action ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/)). Incumbents are absorbing the category; Salesforce completed its acquisition of Qualified, an inbound AI SDR, on 1 April 2026 ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2025/12/16/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-qualified/)).

Published benchmarks set a sobering baseline and depend heavily on the denominator. Instantly's 2026 benchmark reports a mean reply rate of **3.43%**, with 58% of replies coming from the first email ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/)). Smartlead's analysis of 850M+ emails finds a median of **135 contacts per reply (0.74%)**, better results for first emails under 50 words, and a 2% bounce danger line ([Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)). Belkins, which excludes auto-replies, reports 0.45% ([lemlist compilation](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)). The numbers that favour signal-timed outreach come from vendors. Unify reports a **9.1% positive reply rate** on product-usage signals ([Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/blog/series-b)), and a vendor guide cites 18% for signal-specific personalisation against 3.4% for generic copy ([Autobound](https://autobound.ai/blog/signal-based-selling-complete-guide)). No credible public study isolates the lift from multichannel orchestration, so the claim that multichannel doubles replies is a hypothesis for SolutionAI to A/B test, not a fact to plan around.

The failures are well documented and remarkably consistent. TechCrunch reported in March 2025 that 11x displayed ZoomInfo and Airtable as customers after trials that never converted. A former employee said the company might report **$14M ARR when contracts past the three-month break clause totalled about $3M**, and ex-staff described 70–80% churn, which 11x disputed with a 79% retention figure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have/)). Its founder-CEO stepped down in May 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/05/11x-ceo-hasan-sukkar-steps-down/)). By May 2026 his successor was saying AI SDRs do not work in their present form, and 11x was screening out buyers without a mature sales organisation ([GTM Council](https://gtmcouncil.substack.com/p/prabhav-jain-ceo-11x-on-stacked-gtm)). Artisan's CEO admitted severe hallucinations at launch and now claims about one per 10,000 emails, using rigid prompts packed with supplied facts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans/)). LinkedIn restricted Artisan in December 2025 for using data brokers that had scraped LinkedIn ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/yes-linkedin-banned-ai-agent-startup-artisan-but-now-its-back)), and Artisan's own SumUp case study shows **976,000+ emails producing about 15 positive replies a week, at $60 per lead** ([Artisan](https://www.artisan.co/customers/sumup-case-study)). Trustpilot reviewers report spending $9,000 on 11x for zero meetings, and a 0.07% positive-response rate on Artisan ([Trustpilot 11x](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/11x.ai); [Trustpilot Artisan](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/artisan.co)). A widely repeated claim of 50–70% annual churn, attributed to UserGems, does not appear in the UserGems article ([UserGems](https://www.usergems.com/blog/are-ai-sdrs-worth-it)). A compiled, low-confidence dataset claims that 47% of AI SDR deployments hit a domain-reputation wall within 90 days, and that pods mixing humans and AI beat pure AI on positive replies, 1.4% against 0.9% ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-sdr-statistics-2026-outbound-sales-data-points)). Agent-reliability research points the same way. On Salesforce's CRMArena-Pro benchmark, the best LLM agents succeed on about 58% of single-turn business tasks but only **35% of multi-turn tasks**, with almost no awareness of confidentiality ([alphaXiv](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2505.18878v1)).

The survivors share recognisable habits. They default to human approval and let autonomy be earned per segment. HubSpot lets reps review drafts before switching to fully autonomous mode, Instantly offers either human-in-the-loop or autopilot reply handling, and Relevance AI formalises an L1–L4 autonomy ladder ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/ai-prospecting-agent); [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/ai-reply-agent); [Relevance AI](https://relevanceai.com/)). Microsoft's documentation, by contrast, says its preview Sales Development agent keeps running on its own until someone stops it and sends email at any hour, weekends included ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/sales/sales-dev-agent/sales-development-agent-overview)). The survivors also ground generation in supplied facts, trigger on signals rather than lists, favour inbound speed-to-lead, screen their own customers and own the deliverability stack. Recipients ask for the same things. On Hacker News one commenter said they would accept outreach only if it showed why each personalised element was relevant ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46733696)). A developer who received two self-disclosed agent emails proposed four norms: disclose the agent, cite the public source, send one message unless the recipient replies, and avoid fake familiarity ([DB Pro](https://www.dbpro.app/blog/i-posted-on-hacker-news-two-ai-agents-emailed-me)). Common Room states the post-flood philosophy most clearly: decide whether to engage before deciding what to say. It also cites Gartner's projection that by 2030, 75% of B2B buyers will prefer sales experiences that put human interaction first ([Common Room](https://www.commonroom.io/blog/ai-sdr/)).

None of this is built for India. No outreach vendor documents Hindi or any other Indian language, and Microsoft's agent supports 22 languages, none of them Indian ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/sales/sales-dev-agent/sales-development-agent-overview)). WhatsApp support comes in two weak forms. One is personal-number automation exposed to Meta's temporary ban on automated outreach to new contacts, as in lemlist ([lemlist help](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/11623498-use-whatsapp-in-lemlist-campaigns)). The other is Business API access gated to HubSpot's Marketing and Service Hub Professional tiers, capped at 1,000 template messages a month ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/inbox/connect-whatsapp-to-the-conversations-inbox)). Talkdesk and Outreach both stopped domestic outbound calls from Indian numbers on 1 August 2024 ([Talkdesk](https://support.talkdesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/27625422599067--Archive-Product-Notice-Changes-to-Domestic-Outbound-Calls-from-Indian-Numbers-Effective-Aug-1-2024); [Outreach](https://support.outreach.io/hc/en-us/articles/16200555167515)). A July 2026 guide for Indian startups names no Indian-built AI SDR and recommends Apollo. It weighs global agents at $500–3,000 a month against ₹6–10 lakh a year for an Indian business-development rep ([Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/ai-sdr-tools-for-indian-startups)).

## Vernacular voice AI is good, cheap and already commoditising

Voice-agent stacks fall into managed orchestrators (Vapi, Retell, Bland, Synthflow, ElevenLabs Agents), open-source frameworks (LiveKit Agents, Pipecat) and model vendors, with Indian telephony and Indic models forming a separate, essential layer. For Hindi and Hinglish over Indian phone lines, the practical core is Indian. Sarvam's voice agents bundle speech recognition, the LLM and speech synthesis at **₹3.00–3.50 a minute** across 11 Indian languages, with telephony through Vobiz at ₹0.20 per 30-second pulse ([Sarvam pricing](https://docs.sarvam.ai/voice-agents/pricing)). Bolna, Ringg, Gnani and Smallest offer competing orchestration and models. Pipecat ships native Exotel and Plivo connectors plus Sarvam and Smallest services, and LiveKit's end-of-turn detector supports Hindi ([Pipecat](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat); [LiveKit](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)). Global orchestrators charge $0.05–0.31 a minute before Indian telephony, and their pricing pages do not list Hindi ([Vapi](https://vapi.ai/pricing); [Retell](https://www.retellai.com/pricing)).

Quality data is thin but favours Indian models exactly where a sales call happens: noisy, narrowband 8 kHz audio of spontaneous speech. One model card measured commercial systems under a single protocol. It shows Sarvam Saaras-v3 at **23.0% word error on GramVaani phone audio and 15.3% on spontaneous IndicVoices**, against 26.9% and 27.5% for ElevenLabs Scribe-v2, while Scribe wins on read speech mixing Hindi and English (7.6% against 16.5%) ([Trelis/Tara](https://huggingface.co/Trelis/tara)). In a blind listening test run by Josh Talks and published by Sarvam, Bulbul V3 ranked first on 8 kHz phone audio while ElevenLabs v3 led on full-band audio ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/bulbul-v3)). A developer on Retell's forum reported unsatisfactory Hindi pronunciation and number handling with the default providers ([Retell community](https://community.retellai.com/t/integration-of-indian-language-based-tts-and-stt-providers-like-sarvam-ai/2856)). Latency is mostly a configuration problem. A production guide measures chained pipelines at 1.0–1.5 seconds and Vapi or Retell at 900–1,100 ms ([Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-voice-agent-production-guide-2026)). Yet one Vapi user saw **3–4 seconds on live calls** against 575–1,000 ms on the dashboard, traced to endpointing waits, that is, how long the agent waits before deciding the caller has finished speaking ([Vapi support](https://support.vapi.ai/t/27532450/high-latency-problem-in-vapi-what-could-be-the-reason)). A cascaded stack (Indic speech recognition, a fast LLM with a tight prompt, Indic speech synthesis) is the safer default, because the best Indic models are only available as components and qualification calls need reliable tool calls.

The hard constraint is compliant telephony, not models. Twilio permits calls to India only from non-Indian numbers ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/in/voice)). Promotional calls must use a **140-series** number registered on DLT, India's telecom registration ledger for commercial senders. Through Plivo this requires a telemarketer registered in Mumbai or Karnataka, Tata DLT, GST registration, timestamped consent records and **about 5–10 business days** of provisioning ([Plivo](https://plivo.com/docs/voice/concepts/140-series-provisioning)). On a Sarvam pay-as-you-go stack with Vobiz telephony and 18% GST, an all-in minute costs about ₹4.6. Assuming 2.5-minute connected calls and a 10–20% qualification rate (assumptions, since no Indian B2B benchmark exists), a qualified lead costs roughly **₹58–115**, against ₹7–8 a minute plus overheads for human callers ([Sarvam](https://docs.sarvam.ai/voice-agents/pricing); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/the-unit-economics-of-indias-voice-ai-boom/)). Bolna's founder puts break-even at **60,000–90,000 connected minutes per use case** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/the-unit-economics-of-indias-voice-ai-boom/)), so at low volume fixed compliance and QA costs dominate.

Capital and competition both point to commoditisation of the application layer. Infrastructure is richly valued: ElevenLabs raised $500M at $11B in February 2026 ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/fr/news/elevenlabs-500m-funding/)), LiveKit $100M at $1B ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/livekit-100-million-series-c/)), and Sarvam a $234M first close at $1.5B ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/sarvam-raises-234m-in-first-close-of-300m-series-b-at-15b-valuation-155498.html)). Indian application players, by contrast, are raising seed and Series A rounds, including Bolna's $6.3M ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/voice-ai-startup-bolna-raises-usd-63-mn-funding-led-by/502062)) and Ringg's roughly $15.5M ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/08/voice-ai-startup-ringg-ai-raises-10m-series-a-peak-xv-partners)). TechCrunch's August 2026 Ringg coverage described outbound calling and lead qualification as low-stickiness, price-driven work, with value shifting to whoever owns the customer relationship and the outcome ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/indias-ringg-gets-backing-from-peak-xv-as-it-pushes-voice-ai-past-the-phone-call/)). Phones are also acquiring AI gatekeepers. iOS 26 asks unknown callers for their name and reason before the phone rings ([Hiya](https://blog.hiya.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-apples-ios-26-call-screening)), and Truecaller's Assistant screens calls in Hindi and Hinglish ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/truecaller-introduces-ai-powered-assistant-that-can-take-calls-on-your-behalf-filter-spam/1675252/)). Equal AI raised $30M in June 2026; it screens calls in 10+ Indian languages for more than 1M monthly users and plans to place outbound calls on users' behalf ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/equal-ai-raises-30m-to-screen-calls-so-indians-dont-have-to/)). Truecaller counted **4,168 crore spam calls** in India in 2025 ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/475283/india-received-4168-crore-spam-calls-2025-truecaller.html)).

Disclosing that the caller is an AI is a genuine trade-off. In a field experiment with more than 6,200 customers, revealing a chatbot before the conversation cut purchases by 79.7%, an effect reduced by disclosing later ([Luo et al., Marketing Science](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v38y2019i6p937-947.html)). But India has no rule requiring AI disclosure on calls, the US FCC treats AI voices as artificial voices that need prior consent ([FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)), and the FTC sued Air AI in August 2025 partly for marketing its agent as a replacement for human sales staff ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund)). On a screened, regulated channel, the right design states identity and reason in the first sentence, which a screening assistant passes on to the person anyway.

## India's incumbents sell lists, and founders resent the calls

India's company-data market is built almost entirely on repackaged MCA filings, and none of its players turns a new company into a needs-qualified, permitted conversation. Investor-intelligence platforms are small and flat: Tracxn, the only listed one, earned ₹84.5 Cr in FY25 (+2%) and posted a net loss ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/tracxn-slips-into-losses-in-q4-fy25-amid-flat-revenue-9307883)). Filings repackagers charge per company. Tofler starts at ₹15,000 a year ([Tofler](https://www.tofler.in/price-and-plans)), and Probe42 says it cleans 743 public sources with more than 200 curators ([Probe42](https://probe42.in/pricing)). Company-verification (KYB) and MCA APIs are cheap and charged per call. Infyner publishes ₹0.10 for a company lookup, ₹2 for directors and ₹5 for a full master profile ([Infyner](https://www.infyner.com/api-pricing)), and Perfios, which owns Karza, crossed ₹700 Cr of FY25 revenue serving lenders ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/perfios-fy25-profit-soars-46-to-₹104-cr-revenue-crosses-₹700-cr/)). The most direct seller of birth leads is InstaFinancials' CompanyFresh. Every working day before 10:30 AM it emails lists of newly incorporated companies with director names, email addresses and mobile numbers, at a list price of ₹60,000 + GST a month (offered at ₹15,000 for Diwali 2025) ([InstaFinancials](https://contact.instafinancials.com/offers/companyfresh-diwali-25-offer)). Its DirectorConnect product returns the mobile number tied to any of India's more than 30 lakh director identification numbers (DINs), with no stated source or consent basis ([InstaFinancials DirectorConnect](https://testing.instafinancials.com/insta-tools/Products/DirectorConnect.html)). No official MCA bulk-data licence was found to exist.

Indian CRMs record inbound leads rather than discovering new ones. LeadSquared, the Indian-built leader, grew FY25 revenue 17% to ₹326 Cr while cutting its loss to ₹89 Cr, and sells marketing accounts from ₹90,000 a year ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/leadsquared-trims-losses-by-45-in-fy25-growth-remains-modest-10561373); [LeadSquared](https://www.leadsquared.com/pricing/)). Zoho CRM is free for three users, Bigin costs $7–18 per user per month, and Kylas offers a free 10,000-record tier with IndiaMART and Justdial lead capture ([Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html); [Bigin](https://www.bigin.com/pricing.html); [Kylas](https://kylas.io/pricing)). Only 14% of last-mile MSMEs use any CRM software ([Storyboard18/PayNearby](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/over-85-of-small-business-owners-using-smartphones-for-biz-ops-report-35559.htm)). WhatsApp economics are set by Meta's per-message pricing, in place since 1 July 2025. India's marketing rate rose from ₹0.7846 to **₹0.8631** on 1 January 2026 ([MSG91](https://msg91.com/guide/whatsapp-pricing-update-2026-and-save-with-msg91)), and utility messages cost about ₹0.115 ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)). Service messages beyond 1,000 a month became chargeable on 1 October 2026, and Indian accounts must migrate to INR billing by 31 December 2026 or stop delivering on 1 January 2027 ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Indian business solution providers (BSPs) add ₹2,399–16,999 a month in subscriptions and land marketing messages at about ₹0.95–1.09 ([Interakt](https://www.interakt.shop/pricing/); [AiSensy](https://aisensy.com/pricing); [Gallabox](https://gallabox.com/pricing)). Gupshup, the scale player, reports about $350M of FY26 revenue and profitability, yet Fidelity marked it down to $278M in January 2026 from $1.4B in 2021 as Meta's own Business Agent tier pushes the platform into the AI layer where BSPs hoped to build ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/gupshup-ai-rebuild-can-it-stay-ahead-of-the-platforms-it-runs-on/)). SolutionAI should keep agent logic in-house and use any BSP as a pipe.

The incumbent way of reaching new founders is speed without relevance. The evidence of resentment is anecdotal but has been consistent for 14 years. A May 2026 investigation names nine banks, including HDFC, ICICI, Kotak and IDFC First, that call founders **within 24 hours of incorporation**, often before the certificate arrives, alongside small compliance intermediaries and a phishing-style email posing as MCA support ([Inventiva](https://www.inventiva.co.in/?p=439177)). The same reporting cites an MCA reply to a Right to Information (RTI) request: MCA's master-data service carries director names and emails but **no phone numbers**. MCA has opened an internal investigation into leakage ([Inventiva](https://www.inventiva.co.in/?p=439181)), and a security researcher told Moneylife that an MCA vulnerability exposed directors' contact data and took 11 months to fix ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/mca-leaked-personal-data-of-indias-richest-people-and-company-directors-says-security-expert/73167.html)). IndiaFilings holds **2.8/5 on Trustpilot** across 1,175 reviews, and autumn 2026 reviewers describe daily calls until payment and a flood of WhatsApp messages and emails ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/indiafilings.com)). Trustpilot withholds Vakilsearch's score after removing fake reviews ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/vakilsearch.com)), and one complainant said Vakilsearch's calls and emails stopped the moment payment cleared ([Voxya](https://voxya.com/company/vakilsearch-complaints/1223319)). MCA itself warns that it never contacts companies by phone or WhatsApp about non-compliance ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/beware-fraudulent-communications-impersonating-mca-officials.html)), so any engine that sounds official will be mistaken for fraud. Razorpay Rize is the instructive counterexample: it acquires founders through communities and programmes rather than cold lists, and claims 14,950+ registrations ([Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/)).

## Thirty thousand company births a month, each with a dated to-do list

India creates roughly **25,000–35,000 new companies and LLPs every month**, a cohort small enough to give every entity its own needs dossier. QwikFilings' analysis of MCA data averages about 18,000 companies and 8,600 LLPs a month across January–July 2026, with 27,817 entities in May, a dip to 15,757 in June and 25,865 in July ([QwikFilings May](https://qwikfilings.com/india-company-incorporation-statistics-may-2026/); [QwikFilings June](https://qwikfilings.com/?p=381591); [QwikFilings July](https://qwikfilings.com/india-company-incorporation-statistics-july-2026/)). SolutionAI's own download of MCA's September 2026 file shows 24,325 companies and about 10,877 LLPs ([UP pipeline L1 report](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L1_registry/REPORT.md)). InstaFinancials and Probe42 publish materially different counts for the same months ([InstaFinancials](https://blog.instafinancials.com/2026/04/02/march-2026-company-incorporation); [Probe42](https://resources.probe42.in/industry-insights/newsletter/the-probe42-newsletter-january-2026-2/)), which is itself evidence that Indian company-data quality is unsolved. **Uttar Pradesh is consistently second** in new incorporations behind Maharashtra: QwikFilings counts 2,109 in May 2026 and 1,760 in July. SolutionAI's own MCA slice runs at 2,189–2,829 UP companies a month, about 11% of India, of which roughly 480–630 look startup-like by activity code ([UP monthly stats](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L1_registry/up_monthly_stats.csv)). DPIIT recognised a record **55,200 startups in FY26**, up 51.6%, taking the total above 2.23 lakh ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/centre-recognises-record-55200-startups-in-fy26/)). The Udyam MSME registry passed **8.84 crore** registrations by 30 June 2026 ([KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/msme/over-884-crore-msmes-registered-on-udyam-platforms-mos-karandlaje)); 98.9% are micro enterprises, and UP is second with 59 lakh ([MSME Dashboard](https://dashboard.msme.gov.in/Udyam_Statewise.aspx)). Tier-2 and tier-3 cities account for more than 45% of recognised startups ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/union-budget/story/economic-survey-2024-indias-start-up-boom-dominated-by-tier-2-tier-3-cities-with-45-of-start-ups-438185-2024-07-22)). Channel and language data favour WhatsApp and vernacular, but the language evidence is a proxy. **97% of last-mile MSMEs use WhatsApp** or WhatsApp Business ([Storyboard18/PayNearby](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/over-85-of-small-business-owners-using-smartphones-for-biz-ops-report-35559.htm)), and 57% of urban internet users prefer regional-language content ([The Week/IAMAI-Kantar](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/01/16/dcm51-biz-iamai-internet-usage-report.html)), but no survey of founders' language preference was found. Hindi-first is a reasonable bet for UP, not a measured fact.

What a new company needs is unusually predictable, because it is written in law and dated from the incorporation date in the public registry. A new private limited company must hold its first board meeting and appoint an auditor within 30 days, issue share certificates within 60 days, and file INC-20A (commencement of business) within 180 days. Missing INC-20A costs ₹50,000 for the company and up to ₹1 lakh per director ([IndiaCorporates](https://indiacorporates.com/blog/company-registration-compliances/)). GST becomes mandatory at turnover thresholds, or from day one for interstate and e-commerce sellers. A compliance-software vendor's study counts more than 1,450 annual obligations costing ₹13–17 lakh a year for a single-unit manufacturing MSME ([Tribune/ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/regulatory-compliance-costs-weigh-heavily-on-small-businesses-annual-burden-hits-rs-13-17-lakh-report/)). Scheme penetration is tiny. Cumulative Section 80-IAC tax-holiday approvals stand at about 3,700 ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/dpiit-clears-187-startups-for-100-tax-relief/491763)), roughly 2% of recognised startups. The Startup India Seed Fund had funded about 2,490 startups through incubators by October 2024 ([Sansad](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/1931270/1/AU2526.pdf)), and its ₹945 Cr corpus is now reported fully committed ([Adda247](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/india-recognises-55200-startups-in-fy26-highest-ever-under-startup-india-initiative/)). An older LocalCircles survey found that only about 21% of startups and MSMEs had benefited from any scheme in 2020 ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/article/companies/21-startups-and-msmes-benefited-from-govt-schemes-in-2020-survey-121011600545_1.html)), while the free government portal myScheme already lists 4,000+ schemes ([Vikaspedia](https://egovernance.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/e-governance/digital-india/myscheme-national-platform?lgn=en)). Visible spend on online compliance platforms is modest: Clear's FY25 revenue was ₹272 Cr ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/clear-reports-rs-272-crore-revenue-and-rs-96-crore-loss-in-fy25-10808038)), which implies most rupees still flow to local chartered accountants (CAs) and company secretaries (CSs). Three implications follow. The strongest intent signal for a new Indian company is a statutory deadline, not a website visit. An honest eligibility-and-readiness service beats a free-money grant pitch. And the path to revenue runs through the CA and CS partners who already capture the spend.

Four interpretive points carry most of the weight. **There is no legitimate-interests basis.** The DPDP Act allows processing only with consent or for a short list of legitimate uses, none of which covers direct marketing ([DPDP Act s.7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). Cold outreach to an identifiable founder therefore depends on the s.3(c)(ii) exclusion for the specific data item, or on consent that does not yet exist.

**The publicly-available exclusion is narrower than it looks.** It plausibly covers director fields that company law requires to be published and contacts founders publish themselves. But a minister told Parliament in August 2024 that scraping public data still requires DPDP compliance, and commentators see a direct contradiction with the statute ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-)). Practitioners warn firms not to rely on the exclusion without verifying how each item entered the public domain ([Spice Route Legal via law.asia](https://law.asia/publicly-available-data-dpdpa/)). The practical risk ranking runs from company role inboxes (lowest), through MCA-mandated director names and DINs and founders' self-published contacts (low), and founder data published by incubators or state portals (medium), to personal mobiles and emails from brokers or scraped directories (high).

**The fiduciary role depends on the business model.** If SolutionAI runs the engine for its own sales, it is the Data Fiduciary. If it runs the engine for clients, they are fiduciaries and SolutionAI is a processor under contract. If it builds a pooled founder database reused across clients, it becomes a fiduciary in its own right.

**The first touch has to stay off the phone network.** TRAI's text contains no B2B exemption and founders' mobiles are personal lines, so every Indian number should be treated as covered. The first touch belongs on email or another non-telecom channel, with calls and SMS following only after consent, ideally captured through an operator's OTP-verified Digital Consent Acquisition facility ([TRAI](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)). Enforcement is heavy and current: TRAI issued more than 731,000 notices to unregistered telemarketers in 2025 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-trai-issued-731000-notices-unregistered-telemarketers-2025/)), and about 21 lakh connections have been disconnected since August 2024 ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dot-and-trai-have-taken-widespread-action-for-curbing-spam-calls/)).

The design follows directly, and it rests on five controls. The first is a provenance and lawful-basis register for every person-level field, in which fields tagged "none" are never used for outreach. The second is an append-only consent ledger that records the notice version shown, the purposes, channels and language consented to, the proof (an OTP, a DCA reference or a form payload) and any withdrawal. The third is a single global suppression list, checked synchronously before every send or dial: an opt-out on any channel suppresses all marketing channels, and the 90-day no-re-ask window is enforced. The fourth is channel gating. Cold email goes only to role addresses or addresses that qualify for the exclusion, WhatsApp only after an opt-in naming SolutionAI, and voice only over 140-series routes to consented numbers. The fifth is an erasure split that deletes the profile on opt-out but keeps a minimal hashed suppression and audit record, which reconciles DPDP's erasure duty with its one-year log retention. The engine should also be built to interoperate with registered consent managers once that framework starts on 13 November 2026.

## Ten architecture lessons converge on a registry-native, consent-gated design

The engineering patterns behind best-in-class lead engines are now well understood. The genuinely hard parts are Indian-specific: resolving Indian entities, encoding channel compliance as code, and measuring incrementality from day one.

**The first lesson is that discovery must be registry-first, not web-first.** Every global product keys on domain or email, but SolutionAI's primary key at birth should be the CIN or LLPIN. The 21-character CIN encodes listing status, industry code, state, year and class ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/what-is-cin)). The PAN is the hub of the identifier graph, because characters 3–12 of every GSTIN are the entity's PAN ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstin-goods-and-service-tax-identification-number-structure.html)), with Udyam numbers, director DINs, domains and phones attaching as they appear. The national spine is the MCA monthly file. It is free and official, appears in the first week of the following month, and covers every new CIN. But it has only six columns, with no address, email or directors, and it can only be downloaded by hand because MCA's Akamai edge blocks scripted clients. data.gov.in's copy of the master data is stale, ending at 3 November 2023 ([UP pipeline L1 report](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L1_registry/REPORT.md); [data.gov.in](https://www.data.gov.in/resource/registrars-companies-roc-wise-company-master-data)). Per-company master data from KYB APIs fills in directors and addresses for ₹0.10–₹5 a call ([Infyner](https://www.infyner.com/api-pricing)). DPIIT's anonymous statistics endpoint (238,177 recognised startups on 1 October 2026) and the Startup in UP directory (2,157 profiles, with emails and phones hidden in the page markup) add startup signals without exposing contacts ([L2 report](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L2_dpiit/REPORT.md); [L3 report](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L3_startinup/REPORT.md)).

**The second lesson is to treat the web as a change-detected evidence layer.** Crawl politely under RFC 9309, which means caching robots.txt for no more than 24 hours and treating a server error on robots.txt as a full disallow ([RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html)). A managed scrape costs about $0.001 a page, and LLM JSON extraction adds 4 credits a page, roughly five times that ([Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)). So run extraction only on pages whose change-tracking status is new or changed ([Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/features/change-tracking)), store raw HTML by content hash so pages can be re-extracted without re-crawling, and retry discovery of a company's domain at day 7, 30 and 90. **The third lesson is to enrich through per-field waterfalls** that copy Clay's billing logic, in which steps that return or validate data cost money and an empty waterfall costs nothing ([Clay FAQ](https://www.clay.com/faq/do-unsuccessful-searches-consume-data-credits-what-about-actions)). Providers should be ordered by cost per validated hit measured on an Indian sample, not by list price, with Indian sources first and Apollo, PDL or Lusha entering only after a domain exists. Negative results should be cached too, and enrichment should be lazy, run only after scoring has cleared a lead for outreach.

**The fourth lesson is tiered entity resolution.** Start with deterministic joins on government keys. Next comes probabilistic scoring with Splink, an implementation of the Fellegi–Sunter model, which links a million records in about a minute on a laptop and down-weights common tokens such as "Solutions" or "Technologies" so they cannot inflate a match ([Splink](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/index.html)). Only grey-zone pairs then go to an LLM adjudicator; with few or no examples, LLMs now match models fine-tuned on thousands of labels and are more robust to unseen entities ([Peeters et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244)). Because generic names in the same city cause false merges, auto-merging should require agreement on at least one non-name field (a director, address or domain). Splink is preferable to Zingg, whose AGPL v3 licence is a risk for a SaaS product ([Zingg](https://github.com/zinggAI/zingg)). **The fifth lesson is to turn every change into a typed event.** Registry diffs (capital increase, director added, charge created, GST registration in a new state) and statutory-clock events computed from the incorporation date (day 30, day 60, day 180, GST thresholds) should feed the decision layer. News can come through GDELT's 15-minute feed ([GDELT](https://www.gdeltproject.org/)) and Inc42's WordPress REST API, while first-party engagement re-scores a lead within minutes.

**The sixth lesson is to score with transparent rules plus a binary LLM rubric first, and a calibrated model later.** Verbalised LLM confidence is poorly calibrated ([arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)), so LLM judgements are features, not probabilities. Once 300–500 outcomes exist, fit logistic regression or gradient-boosted trees and calibrate them on held-out data. Hold out **5–10% of eligible leads at random** from day one; it is the only way to estimate uplift later and to find the "sleeping dogs" who respond worse when contacted ([Uplift modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uplift_modelling)). **The seventh lesson is to separate durable business workflows from agent reasoning.** Temporal gives replayable audit trails but caps history at 51,200 events, which forces a continue-as-new design ([Temporal](https://docs.temporal.io/workflow-execution/limits)). Inngest's keyed throttles map naturally to pacing per mailbox and per WhatsApp number ([Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/docs/guides/throttling)). Research, drafting and reply classification should run as bounded calls through the LLM gateway, and human approval should be a durable wait. LangGraph's interrupts support this, but a paused node restarts from the beginning on resume, so earlier side effects must be idempotent ([LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/javascript/langgraph/interrupts)). The judge of copy quality should be aligned to one domain expert using binary pass/fail criteria and about 100 labels per failure mode ([Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/)). Deterministic checks (consent, suppression, quiet hours, frequency caps) should run last, immediately before every send.

**The eighth lesson is to engineer channels to the providers' rules.** Never send cold mail from SolutionAI's primary domains. Instead, run a pool of outbound domains with SPF, 2048-bit DKIM and DMARC, two or three mailboxes per domain, and auto-pause any mailbox whose bounces exceed 2% ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). WhatsApp should run on the Cloud API, now the only option, with one WhatsApp Business Account per tenant if the engine ever serves clients, so that one tenant's campaign cannot throttle the others. The engine must also respect the business portfolio's unique-recipient limit, which steps from 250 to 2,000, 10,000, 100,000 and unlimited ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/messaging-limits)), and back off for at least 24 hours when Meta's undisclosed per-user marketing cap returns error 131049 ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/marketing-templates/per-user-limits.md)). **The ninth lesson is to keep the golden record in MongoDB** with embedded provenance and co-located vector search ([MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/atlas-vector-search/vector-search-overview/)). Activities, consents and signals should be append-only and replicated into a columnar warehouse for funnels, model training and attribution. Attribution should be measured by incrementality against holdouts, the direction Google Analytics took when it dropped rule-based multi-touch models in November 2023 ([GA4](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10596866)). **The tenth lesson is to trace each lead's journey end to end** with OpenTelemetry spans carrying token and cost attributes, sent to Langfuse ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/docs/opentelemetry/get-started)), backed by golden datasets for entity resolution, extraction, judge quality and reply intent that run as CI evals on every model or prompt change.

The unit costs confirm that money is not the binding constraint; permission is. Enriching every new UP company (about 2,500 a month) at ₹5 each costs about ₹12,500 a month, and enriching 25,000 companies nationally costs about ₹1.25 lakh. These numbers are trivial next to the cost of losing a sending domain, a WhatsApp business portfolio or a telecom connection. Consent, deliverability and compliance are what actually limit volume.

The top opportunity wins because every independent line of evidence converges on it. The need is legally real and dated from public data, the incumbents' alternative is resented, regulation is making the alternative expensive, and a message carrying a legal citation and a date is verifiable by its recipient, which answers the trust deficit directly. Its weakness is also real: a lawful first-touch channel has to be designed, and myScheme gives eligibility search away free. So the edge must come from personalisation by CIN, honesty (including "you do not qualify, and here is why") and a clean handoff to fulfilment, not from the eligibility list itself. The birth graph ranks second as an enabler rather than a product, because list aggregators exist and Clay's success suggests value accrues to orchestration, not to owning one source. The CA/CS loop ranks third because it is where founders' rupees already flow, and tracking a service through to the filing receipt answers the most common complaint about online providers: that they go quiet once paid. The consented introductions marketplace is the boldest idea. It would invert the leaked-list economy by letting founders opt into specific categories (a bank account, accounting software, insurance, a website) for a bounded window while vendors pay per accepted introduction. The demand side is evidenced by banks' behaviour and list prices, but the mechanism is untested and legally heavier, so it belongs after the first-party engine has earned trust.

## The thesis: a permission engine for company births

SolutionAI should define a new category, **permissioned company-birth engagement**, rather than enter list-selling, AI SDRs or voice bots, all of which are crowded and commoditising. The promise to a founder is simple. Within days of incorporation you hear from one trustworthy source, in your language, with a dated and cited plan for your first 180 days and an honest view of what you qualify for. Nobody calls you unless you asked. When you need a CA or CS, the one you get is verified and accountable to a filing receipt. The promise to the CEO is a lead engine whose output is consented founders and paid, fulfilled services rather than messages sent. The thesis is differentiated because each pillar rests on an asset global players lack and Indian list-sellers have no incentive to build. It also extends a posture SolutionAI has already adopted: the existing design limits WhatsApp to opted-in contacts and AI calling to registered TRAI-compliant channels, and the UP pipeline deliberately did not capture contact fields hidden on state portals ([L3 report](file:///C:/Users/goura/Downloads/up-startup-intel/raw/L3_startinup/REPORT.md)).

The thesis has six pillars. The first is a **registry-native birth graph** that extends the UP Startup Atlas to the full national MCA file at no extra data cost, keys every entity on CIN or LLPIN, and attaches PAN, GSTINs, Udyam and DIN-linked directors as they appear. The second is **needs inference from the statutory clock** combined with SolutionAI's existing scheme matcher, so that every recommendation carries a legal citation and a date, and "not eligible, and here is why" is a valid answer. The third is a **restraint engine** with a contact budget, a default of not sending, logged reasons and a random holdout. The fourth is a **consent-first sequence**. The first touch reaches only lawful, low-risk addresses, or arrives through inbound partners such as Mr. AI users, CA partners, incubators, click-to-WhatsApp ads and campus sign-ups. It delivers a value artifact, such as a personalised compliance calendar in Hindi or English, and accepting it is the consent event. That consent unlocks WhatsApp utility reminders, in-app notifications and, for founders who ask, a Hindi or English voice call over a registered 140-series route, with AI disclosure in the opening sentence and a human handoff. The fifth is the **fulfilment loop** through the verified CA/CS marketplace, whose outcomes feed back into scoring, field verification and partner ratings; this consented flow is SolutionAI's equivalent of ZoomInfo's contributory network and Apollo's engagement-based verification. The sixth is **agent-native access**: an MCP server over the graph and its actions, so a founder can ask Mr. AI what is due this month, partners can query consented leads, and the voice agent's opening line is legible to Equal AI, Truecaller and iOS screeners. The consented introductions marketplace is the category-defining extension, but it should follow the first-party engine and a counsel review of SolutionAI's fiduciary role, not precede them.

Sequencing should follow the regulatory calendar.

| By | Milestone | Scope and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| 13 November 2026 | Provenance register, consent ledger, suppression service and Hindi/English notices in place | Outreach limited to UP and to SolutionAI's own offers, on email and in-app channels; WhatsApp only for opted-in founders through a BSP; voice limited to inbound calls and requested callbacks |
| 31 December 2026 | WhatsApp billing migrated to INR | If the founder approves outbound voice, the 5–10-day 140-series onboarding completed through a registered telemarketer |
| Early 2027 | Graph scaled nationally | Scoring moved to a calibrated model once 300–500 outcomes exist; introductions piloted with two or three partner categories |
| 13 May 2027 | Full DPDP rights, retention and breach processes running | Applies across all channels and partners |

Success should be measured by consented founders, positive replies, paid conversions and on-time filings, with complaint rates as hard constraints and incremental lift over the holdout as the deciding metric.

### Where the evidence is thin

The weakest part of this research is social-media voice of customer. Reddit could not be reached at all, because both search and fetch were refused, and the shared budget of 200 searches per turn ran out before LinkedIn, X, YouTube, G2 or Product Hunt could be mined. Sentiment about global vendors therefore rests on Trustpilot, Hacker News and competitors' blogs, and Indian founder sentiment rests on Trustpilot, complaint sites, Quora and press investigations, none of which are representative samples. Several things are simply unknown. No survey was found on Indian founders' language preference, on how many calls a new director receives, or on how founders feel about AI calls. No benchmark exists for WhatsApp B2B reply rates, AI-call connection rates or qualification rates in India. And there is no direct evidence that CAs or founders will pay for a value-first engine. Several regulatory points also remain open: the content of TRAI's Third Amendment of 18 September 2026, whether Telecom Act s.28 has commenced, the conflicting DPDP date for ss.9–10, and MCA's terms of use, which returned HTTP 403. Most figures on AI SDR performance, voice quality and signal lift are vendor-reported, and the only Hindi speech-recognition comparison measured under a common protocol is a single model card. Before committing engineering time, SolutionAI should run a manual sentiment pass on r/StartUpIndia, r/IndiaBusiness, r/CAIndia and LinkedIn, and hold 15–20 founder interviews in Hindi and English with recently incorporated UP companies.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that the scarce input in lead generation is no longer contact data; it is permission and proof of outcome. Clay's rise and ZoomInfo's stall show data commoditising, the AI SDR collapse shows that cheap sending is a liability, and India's own voice and WhatsApp infrastructure is commoditising fast enough that the press already calls it low-stickiness. What remains scarce is knowing a company exists before anyone else's tools can see it, holding the right to talk to it, and holding evidence that talking helped. SolutionAI already has unusual raw material for all three: a working MCA ingestion pipeline, Mr. AI and grant matching as engines of value worth consenting to, and a CA/CS marketplace that can verify outcomes down to a filing receipt. That combination does not exist in Bengaluru's voice startups, in Indian list-sellers or in San Francisco's AI SDRs.

The second takeaway is timing. Indian regulation is turning the incumbents' speed advantage into a liability on a published schedule: consent managers from 13 November 2026, INR WhatsApp billing from 31 December 2026, and full DPDP duties from 13 May 2027. An engine built in the next seven months with consent at its core will be compliant on the day competitors start retrofitting, while one built as an Indian 11x would need re-architecting within a year. The first version should therefore be judged not by how many founders it reaches but by how many say yes, with the random holdout showing whether they would have said yes anyway.
