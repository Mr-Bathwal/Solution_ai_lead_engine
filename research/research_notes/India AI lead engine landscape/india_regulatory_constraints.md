# India: legal and regulatory constraints on an AI lead engine (company/founder data plus automated email, WhatsApp, SMS and AI voice outreach)

*Research notes, current to 24 October 2026. This is a research briefing, not legal advice. Primary texts read in full: the DPDP Act 2023 (MeitY PDF), the DPDP Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)), the TRAI TCCCPR Second Amendment Regulations 2025 and TRAI Press Release 11/2025, the Telecommunications Act 2023, the IT Act 2000 (India Code), the FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17, the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy, the Meta Terms for the WhatsApp Business Platform and the LinkedIn User Agreement. Caveat: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Points I could not verify against a source are listed under Gaps, not stated as fact. The MCA21 site and the TRAI CMS host refused automated access, and the MCA terms of use could not be read.*

## Q1. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: definitions, consent and notice, legitimate uses, commencement, penalties, Board status and processors

### Takeaway
The DPDP Act is law, but almost none of its substantive duties bind private businesses yet. Consent, notice, security, erasure and penalties start on **13 May 2027**, and the consent-manager framework starts on **13 November 2026**. The Data Protection Board exists on paper but had no Chairperson or Members as of August 2026. Once those duties start, the Act gives **no "legitimate interests" or direct-marketing ground**. Outreach that processes an identifiable founder's personal data needs either consent or the "publicly available" exclusion (Q2).

### Cited Findings
**Texts and dates**
- Parliament enacted the DPDP Act on 11 August 2023 ([PIB factsheet, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)).
- The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified as G.S.R. 846(E), dated "New Delhi, the 13th November, 2025" ([DPDP Rules 2025, Gazette text](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). PIB's factsheet gives the notification date as 14 November 2025 ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)), so dates calculated from it can be off by a day.
- Rule 1 sets out the commencement verbatim: "(2) Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their publication… (3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year after the date of publication… (4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication" ([DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).
- The Act itself was commenced on 13 November 2025. Section 6(9), Section 27(1)(d) and Rule 4 (consent managers) take effect on **13 November 2026**, and the principal provisions on **13 May 2027** ([India Briefing, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).
- MeitY issued the DPDP (Removal of Difficulties) Order 2026 on 5 October 2026, published in the Gazette on 7 October 2026. It makes only textual fixes to Section 9(1) (children and persons with disability) and Section 10(2)(c)(ii) ("data audit") ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-removal-of-difficulties-order-2026-consent-data-audit-47274.html/)). The same article says Sections 9 and 10 take effect on "13 March 2027". That conflicts with the 13 May 2027 date in the Rules and other sources, and I could not resolve it.
- In January 2026 MeitY consulted on cutting the 18-month window to 12 months, which would have moved the deadline to 13 November 2026. Industry comments were due on 4 February 2026, and the ministry said "no final decision has been taken" ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm); [Takshashila, 26 Jan 2026](https://takshashila.org.in/content/blogs/20260126-meity-considers-reducing-dpdpa-compliance-timelines.html); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)). The September 2026 India Briefing article still uses 13 May 2027, which suggests the compression was not adopted ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).

**Definitions and scope**
- Key definitions: a Data Fiduciary decides why and how personal data is processed, a Data Processor processes it on the fiduciary's behalf, a Data Principal is the individual concerned, and a Consent Manager runs an interoperable platform for giving, managing and withdrawing consent ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)).
- The Act applies to digital personal data, and to processing outside India if it is connected with offering goods or services to Data Principals in India ([DPDP Act s.3](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).

**Lawful grounds, consent and notice**
- Section 4(1) allows processing only "(a) for which the Data Principal has given her consent; or (b) for certain legitimate uses" ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- Section 6(1) requires consent to be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action… limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose." Section 6(4) lets the Data Principal withdraw consent at any time, "with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given" ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- The first legitimate use, s.7(a), covers data the Data Principal "has voluntarily provided… to the Data Fiduciary" for a specified purpose and has not objected to. The illustrations are a pharmacy receipt and a broker the person contacted. The other s.7 uses relate to the State, legal obligations, emergencies, employment and similar. None covers direct marketing or legitimate interests ([DPDP Act s.7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- Under Rule 3, the notice must stand on its own and give "an itemised description of such personal data" and "the specified purpose or purposes". It must also give a link and means to withdraw consent (with ease comparable to giving it), exercise rights, and complain to the Board ([DPDP Rules r.3](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). Section 5(3) lets the Data Principal read the notice in English or any Eighth Schedule language ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- Data Fiduciaries must answer access, correction, update and erasure requests within 90 days ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Rule 9 requires the business contact of a DPO, or another person who can answer, to be published prominently ([DPDP Rules r.9](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).

**Consent managers**
- Consent managers register with the Board under Rule 4 and First Schedule Part A, which requires net worth of at least ₹2 crore. They must be companies based in India ([DPDP Rules r.4 and First Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Rule 4 starts on about 13 November 2026.

**Security, breach, retention and erasure (from 13 May 2027)**
- Rule 6 sets minimum safeguards: encryption, obfuscation, masking or tokenisation; access control; access logs and monitoring; backups; **keeping logs for one year**; and security clauses in Data Processor contracts ([DPDP Rules r.6](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).
- Rule 7 requires breach intimation to affected Data Principals and the Board "without delay", with a detailed report to the Board "within seventy-two hours" ([DPDP Rules r.7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).
- Section 8(7) requires erasure when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, and the fiduciary must "cause its Data Processor to erase" ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). Under Rule 8(2), a Third-Schedule class must notify the Data Principal 48 hours before inactivity-based erasure. Rule 8(3) requires keeping personal data, traffic data and processing logs "for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing" for Seventh Schedule purposes ([DPDP Rules r.8](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).

**Data processors (vendors)**
- Section 8(1) makes the Data Fiduciary responsible "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary… in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor". Section 8(2) allows a processor to be engaged "only under a valid contract" ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). The penalty schedule is aimed at Data Fiduciaries (see below). Processors carry obligations through contract and Rule 6(1)(f).

**Penalties (Schedule to s.33)**
- Failing to take reasonable security safeguards: up to ₹250 crore. Failing to notify a breach: up to ₹200 crore. Breaching children's obligations: up to ₹200 crore. Breaching Significant Data Fiduciary obligations: up to ₹150 crore. Breaching Data Principal duties: up to ₹10,000. Any other breach of the Act or Rules: up to ₹50 crore ([DPDP Act Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)).

**Startup exemption**
- Section 17(3) lets the Central Government notify Data Fiduciaries, "including startups", as exempt from s.5 (notice), s.8(3), s.8(7) (erasure), s.10 and s.11 ([DPDP Act s.17(3)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). The DPDP Rules 2025 text contains no startup exemption: a search of the Gazette text found no "startup" provision ([DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)).
- Section 44(2)(a) of the DPDP Act omits IT Act s.43A, the SPDI compensation regime ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). Commentators say the SPDI Rules 2011 stay in force through the 18-month transition ([Mondaq/Legal500](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1708830/dpdp-act-compliance-mandate)).

**Data Protection Board status**
- Section 18 and Rules 17–21, which establish the Board, came into effect on 13 November 2025. As of 1 August 2026 the Board had "no appointed Chairperson and no appointed Members". A Cabinet-Secretary-led search committee (for the Chairperson) and a MeitY-Secretary-led committee (for Members) were still soliciting names, after calls dated 6 May and 6 June 2026 ([LiveLaw, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/india-data-protection-board-established-law-543751)). The Board will be digital-first, with four members and appeals to TDSAT ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)).

### Inferences
- Before 13 May 2027, the DPDP Act creates little direct liability for the lead engine, and the Board cannot yet adjudicate. Telecom (TRAI), platform (Meta, LinkedIn) and IT Act s.43 risks are live **now**. SolutionAI should still build DPDP-grade consent and provenance from day one. Data collected in 2026 will still be held in May 2027, and there is no grandfathering for unlawfully obtained data.
- With no legitimate-interests ground, cold outreach to an identifiable founder (name plus personal email or mobile) has to rely on one of two bases: (a) the s.3(c)(ii) exclusion for that specific data item (Q2), or (b) consent, which by definition does not exist yet for a cold contact. A first-touch message is therefore legally safest when it uses exempt or non-personal data (a company role inbox, or a founder-published business email) and its goal is to **collect consent**, after which the DPDP consent regime applies.
- Role split: if SolutionAI runs the engine for its own sales, it is the Data Fiduciary. If it offers the engine to client MSMEs, each client is likely the Fiduciary and SolutionAI the Processor, needing a s.8(2) contract. But if SolutionAI builds and reuses a pooled founder database across clients, it decides purpose and means and is a Fiduciary in its own right.
- Because Rule 8(3) requires keeping processing logs for at least a year while s.8(7) requires erasure on withdrawal, the design should split data. Erase the contact record and marketing profile on opt-out, and keep a minimal, access-restricted suppression and audit record (e.g. hashed identifier, timestamp, channel).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the S.O. that notified commencement of the Act's sections, so the exact section-by-section list is unverified. The "13 March 2027" date for ss.9–10 is unexplained.
- I found no MeitY FAQ or Board guidance specific to marketing or B2B outreach.
- I found no notification under s.17(3) exempting startups. There may be one I could not search for after the search budget ran out.

## Q2. Does the s.3(c)(ii) "made publicly available" exclusion cover director names and emails in MCA filings or on websites?

### Takeaway
Probably yes, **for the specific fields the law requires to be published** (the MCA registry under the Companies Act). The same applies to data a founder **personally** published (their own website "team" page, DPIIT profile, or public social profile). It is **not** a blanket "anything on the internet" exemption. It is untested, the government has said publicly that scraping public data still requires DPDP compliance, and it does not cover data a third party (directory, data broker, news site) published without a legal duty.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory text: the Act does "not apply to… (ii) personal data that is made or caused to be made publicly available by— (A) the Data Principal to whom such personal data relates; or (B) any other person who is under an obligation under any law for the time being in force in India to make such personal data publicly available." Illustration: "X, an individual, while blogging her views, has publicly made available her personal data on social media. In such case, the provisions of this Act shall not apply." ([DPDP Act s.3(c)(ii)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf))
- On MCA director data: DIR-2 details are a director's personal data. Once the company files e-form DIR-12, "the e-form DIR-12 becomes a public document and can be inspected by any person by paying a nominal fee." That supports the legal-obligation limb. The analysis was written in February 2024, before commencement, and reaches no firm conclusion ([Conventus Law/Clasis Law, 13 Feb 2024](https://conventuslaw.com/?p=112869)).
- In April 2022 the MCA amended the rules so that members' email IDs, PAN, unique identification numbers and addresses in the register of members cannot be inspected or copied. DIR-2 also carries identity documents (PAN, passport, Aadhaar, utility bills), and the author warns they "can be easily misused" ([Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/?p=112869)).
- The government's stance: in August 2024, MoS Jitin Prasada told the Rajya Sabha that scraping public user data is covered by the IT Act, the IT Rules and the DPDP Act, and that consent, transparency and individual rights still apply. An IAPP commentator concludes "The statute and the government's stance simply do not align" and that "public does not mean permissionless" ([IAPP, 30 Oct 2025](https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-)).
- Spice Route Legal says the government's consent position "ignores an important exemption under section 3(c)(ii)… This creates a fundamental contradiction". It adds that the Act "does not define what constitutes 'made publicly available' or clarify whether personal data that was once public but later is made private will continue to be exempt" ([Spice Route Legal via law.asia, 1 Aug 2025](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)).
- Practical hurdles: the exclusion "first requires verification of the source", and automated tools "may fail to verify whether individuals themselves have permitted the publication". Data an employer or news outlet publishes may not count as "caused" by the individual. Firms "ought not to rely on their ability to avail this exemption" and should identify other grounds ([Spice Route Legal via law.asia, 31 Jul 2024](https://law.asia/publicly-available-data-dpdpa/)).
- The test is how the data "entered the public domain", not whether it can be found online. Companies should record provenance and the basis for relying on the exclusion. The Act does not say that aggregation, enrichment or profiling removes data from the exclusion. Lawful collection is a separate question (website terms, IP, unauthorised-access laws) ([India Briefing, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).

### Inferences
- Rough risk ranking for founder data, lowest to highest:
  - **Lowest:** the company's own registered email or role inbox (not personal data if it does not identify an individual).
  - **Low:** director names, DIN and designation from MCA records the law requires to be public (limb B), and contacts a founder published on their own site or in their own public profile (limb A).
  - **Medium:** founder data published by an incubator, accelerator or state portal. Limb A applies only if the founder supplied it for publication; limb B only if a law requires publication.
  - **High:** personal mobile numbers or personal emails from data brokers, enrichment APIs or scraped directories. No exclusion applies, so the full DPDP Act applies from May 2027 and there is no lawful basis without consent.
- Store per-field provenance (source URL or registry, capture date, which limb applies) so each record can show its exclusion basis. Re-verify periodically, because data later made private is an open question.
- The exclusion only takes the data out of the DPDP Act. It does not authorise sending messages: TRAI, WhatsApp and LinkedIn rules apply regardless.

### Gaps
- No court, Board or MeitY ruling interprets s.3(c)(ii) for registry data. I found no MCA statement on third-party marketing use of MCA21 data, and the MCA terms of use could not be fetched (HTTP 403).
- I could not confirm which director fields MCA21 currently shows for free (for example, whether director email or mobile is visible). Earlier filings suggest identity documents are attached to DIR-12, but master-data screens may show only name, DIN and designation.

## Q3. Is B2B cold email legal in India?

### Takeaway
India has **no CAN-SPAM-style anti-spam statute**, and no consent rule specific to email is in force. The one IT Act provision that touched annoying or deceptive email (s.66A) was struck down in 2015. Cold B2B email is therefore not illegal in itself today. Its legal exposure comes from (i) the DPDP Act from 13 May 2027 when the address identifies an individual and is not exempt (Q1–Q2), (ii) IT Act s.43 and s.66 if the addresses were harvested by unauthorised access (Q8), and (iii) fraud or impersonation law for deceptive content.

### Cited Findings
- *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India* (SC, 24 March 2015) declared IT Act s.66A unconstitutional. Section 66A(c) had covered "any electronic mail or electronic mail message for the purpose of causing annoyance or inconvenience" or misleading the addressee about its origin ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110813550/)).
- The Telecommunications Act 2023 s.28 regime for "specified messages" (consent and DND, see Q4) covers telecom messages. The text gives the Central Government rule-making power for user-protection measures "in consonance with any regulations notified by" TRAI ([Telecommunications Act 2023 s.28](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2023/250880.pdf)). I found nothing extending it to internet email.
- Global benchmark: US CAN-SPAM "makes no exception for business-to-business email". It requires truthful headers, an ad disclosure, a valid postal address and opt-outs honoured "within 10 business days", with civil penalties up to $53,088 per email ([FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).

### Inferences
- Adopt CAN-SPAM-equivalent hygiene voluntarily: truthful sender identity, the company's legal name and postal address, a one-click unsubscribe honoured promptly, and no deceptive subject lines. This also prepares for the DPDP requirement (Rule 3) that withdrawal be as easy as giving consent. It helps with Gmail and Outlook bulk-sender deliverability too, though I did not verify current Google or Yahoo rules.
- Prefer company role addresses and founder-published business emails. Treat guessed personal-pattern emails (firstname@company) and enriched personal emails as personal data that needs a lawful basis from May 2027.

### Gaps
- I found no MeitY or draft "anti-spam" rule for email in 2025–2026. I could not search for it once the search budget was exhausted. I also did not verify current Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.

## Q4. TRAI TCCCPR 2018 (as amended 2024–2025) and the Telecommunications Act 2023: SMS, voice, robocalls and AI calls; do the rules cover business numbers?

### Takeaway
All commercial SMS and voice must go through a **DLT-registered sender (Principal Entity)**. Promotional calls must come from the **140-series** (the 1600-series is for transactional and service calls). Templates, headers and consent are registered on DLT. Each promotional message must carry an opt-out. **Auto-dialer and robo-calls (which would include AI voice agents) must be pre-declared in writing to the originating operator.** Calling from ordinary 10-digit numbers makes the sender an "unregistered telemarketer". The consequences are a 15-day bar on all outgoing services, then one-year disconnection across operators and blacklisting. The regulations contain **no B2B exemption**. Enforcement is heavy and active right now.

### Cited Findings
**Instrument and dates**
- The Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference (Second Amendment) Regulations 2025 (1 of 2025) are dated 12 February 2025. They come into force 30 days after Gazette publication, and certain clauses (regs 8, 17, 20(a)–(b), 21(b)) after 60 days ([TRAI Regulation text](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf); [TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)).

**Registration and number series**
- Under regulation 3 as substituted: "Every Access Provider shall ensure that any commercial communication using its network takes place only using registered headers or the number resources allotted to the Senders from special series". Also: "No Sender, who is not registered with any Access Provider… shall make any commercial communication", and such a sender's telecom resources "may be put under suspension or may also be disconnected" ([TRAI Regulation](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- The amended UCC definition treats any commercial communication by an unregistered sender as UCC, and treats messages or calls meant to deceive as UCC ([TRAI Regulation, cl. (bw)](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- The amendment "restricts senders from using normal 10-digit numbers for telemarketing… the 140 series will continue to be used for promotional calls, the newly allocated 1600 series is designated for transactional and service calls" ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)). In a Rajya Sabha answer reported on 10 February 2026, the 1600-series was described as prescribed for financial-sector service and transactional calls, with strict timelines ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dot-and-trai-have-taken-widespread-action-for-curbing-spam-calls/)).
- DLT basics from the TRAI explanatory memorandum:
  - Senders (PEs) and telemarketers must register with an Access Provider. Headers and content templates are registered, and messages are scrubbed against templates.
  - Customers set preferences by category, time band and weekday.
  - The **Digital Consent Acquisition (DCA)** facility records a customer's consent to a specific sender on DLT after OTP verification, and provides for revocation.

  ([TRAI Regulation, Explanatory Memorandum](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf))

**Consent and preference rules**
- Promotional messages may go only to subscribers who have not blocked the category, or who consented. Where the sender holds "explicit digital consent", promotional messages and calls are delivered "irrespective of their preferences registered". Every promotional message must carry an opt-out mechanism, and mixed content counts as promotional ([TRAI Regulation, cl. (au), (av)](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- After an opt-out, a sender may not ask the customer for consent again for **90 days**. Consent for an ongoing transaction is valid for **7 days**. Implicit consent for service and transactional messages lasts only for the duration of the contract. Headers carry the suffixes -P, -S, -T and -G ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)).

**Robocalls and auto-dialers (relevant to AI voice)**
- New regulation 4: "Every Sender shall notify the Originating Access Provider, in advance, about the use of Auto Dialer or Robo-Calls as well as the intended objective of such calls in writing" ([TRAI Regulation](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- In the memorandum, TRAI decided no separate robocall regime was needed. Promotional auto-dialer and robo-calls must use the 140-series (with 1600 for service and transactional calls), DCA platforms should be used for consent to promotional robocalls, and pre-declaration lets operators scrub calls against customer preferences. Operators must also deploy detection for "robotic calls, auto dialer calls or pre-recorded announcements" and AI/ML-based spam detection ([TRAI Regulation, EM paras 46–52](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).

**Penalties and enforcement**
- Senders: a first violation bars outgoing services on all their telecom resources for 15 days. Later violations bring disconnection of all resources, "including PRI/SIP trunks", across all operators for one year, plus blacklisting. Action is triggered by 5 complaints in 10 days, previously 10 in 7. The complaint window is now 7 days (previously 3), and operators must act on unregistered senders within 5 days (previously 30). Operators can require security deposits that are forfeited on violation ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)).
- The earlier 2018 ladder for unregistered telemarketers (UTMs), described in the memorandum, was: a warning; then a six-month usage cap (20 calls and 20 SMS a day); then disconnection for "up to two years" and blacklisting ([TRAI Regulation, EM](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- Operators: financial disincentives of ₹2 lakh, ₹5 lakh and ₹10 lakh per instance for misreporting UCC ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)). TRAI has levied ₹153.8 crore in financial disincentives on operators for improper complaint closures ([Communications Today, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dot-and-trai-have-taken-widespread-action-for-curbing-spam-calls/)).
- On 13 August 2024, TRAI directed operators to disconnect all telecom resources of UTMs making commercial calls. Around 21 lakh connections have been disconnected. More than 3 lakh senders and 21,000 telemarketers are on DLT, and in December 2025 DLT blocked over 7.5 crore SMS and calls a day ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dot-and-trai-have-taken-widespread-action-for-curbing-spam-calls/)).
- 2025 enforcement: over 731,000 notices to UTMs, more than 184,000 telecom resources disconnected, and 89,936 repeat offenders capped. Cumulative disconnections since August 2024 exceed 2.1 million. There were 3.1 million spam complaints in 2025, 1.7 million of them through the DND app ([MediaNama, 23 Feb 2026, citing TRAI PRs 20 and 21 of 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-trai-issued-731000-notices-unregistered-telemarketers-2025/)).

**Business numbers**
- The amended UCC and promotional definitions refer to the "Recipient", "Subscriber" or "Customer" without carving out business subscribers. Sender records classify senders as "individual/Enterprise" ([TRAI Regulation](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)). I found no B2B exemption in the 2025 amending text.

**Telecommunications Act 2023**
- Section 28(1) defines a "specified message" as any message "offering, advertising or promoting goods, services… business opportunity". Section 28(2) lets the Central Government make rules, "in consonance with" TRAI regulations, for (a) prior consent, (b) "Do Not Disturb" registers, and (c) reporting mechanisms ([Telecommunications Act 2023](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2023/250880.pdf)).
- Third Schedule, item 3, for contravening s.28: "First Offence: Civil penalty up to fifty thousand rupees. Each subsequent offence: Civil penalty up to two lakh rupees for each such instance, or suspension of telecommunication service, or a combination thereof" ([Telecommunications Act 2023](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2023/250880.pdf)).

### Inferences
- An AI voice agent that dials prospects is an "auto dialer or robo-call" in substance. It needs a 140-series number through a registered telemarketer, DLT sender registration, written pre-declaration to the operator, and preference scrubbing (or DCA consent). Running it from ordinary SIMs, cloud-telephony 10-digit DIDs or SIP trunks without this risks disconnecting **all** of SolutionAI's telecom resources, including support lines, for a year.
- Founders' mobiles are almost always personal subscriber lines. Even landlines registered to a company get no B2B safe harbour under the text. Treat every Indian number as covered.
- Best practice is to make the first touch by email or another non-telecom channel, collect consent (ideally through DCA or OTP-verified web consent), and only then call or SMS.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Telecommunications Act s.28 and its rules have been brought into force, or whether DoT has notified rules under s.28(2). I recall s.28 being among the sections commenced on 26 June 2024, but I could not verify this.
- The TRAI direction of 13 August 2024 itself (for example, whether it names robocalls or pre-recorded calls and sets a two-year blacklist) was not retrievable.
- MediaNama mentions a March 2026 proposal on charges for "Automated Bulk Calls", which I could not read.
- I found no TRAI document that addresses generative-AI voices specifically.

## Q5. WhatsApp Business: opt-in rules, cold outreach and enforcement

### Takeaway
Cold WhatsApp outreach to scraped or purchased numbers breaks Meta's policy. The person must have **given the number to you** and **opted in to hear from your named business**. Business-initiated messages must use approved templates. Enforcement goes through user blocks and reports, quality-based rate limits, messaging-tier caps and account suspension or termination. Separately, Meta's terms bar general-purpose AI assistants from the platform.

### Cited Findings
- WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (last updated 23 September 2026): "You may only contact people on WhatsApp if: (a) they have given you their mobile phone number or username;" and you have received opt-in permission "from the recipient confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages" ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).
- From the same policy:
  - "You may only initiate conversations using an approved Message Template."
  - "Outside the 24-hour customer service window, you may only send messages via approved Message Templates."
  - "Do not confuse, deceive, defraud, mislead, spam, or surprise people with your communications."
  - "You must respect all requests (either on or off WhatsApp) by a person to block, discontinue, or otherwise opt out."

  ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/))
- Enforcement under the policy: Meta "may limit or remove your access… or limit your ability to send messages". It "may limit the amount of messages a business can send or calls a business can initiate" based on feedback, and "may prohibit you and your organization from all future use of WhatsApp products". Blocks and reports feed enforcement ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).
- Meta developer docs: since the November 2024 policy update, opt-in "can be general and not specifically for WhatsApp, as long as businesses comply with all local laws". The opt-in must state that the person is opting in and must name the business. Methods include SMS, web, IVR and paper. Sustained low quality leads to rate limiting ([Meta, Get opt-in](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in)).
- Messaging limits are counted per business portfolio as unique users messaged outside service windows in 24 hours. Portfolios start at 250, reach 2,000 through a scaling path, then 10,000, 100,000 and unlimited by automatic scaling. Scaling requires high-quality messaging and using at least half the current limit in the last 7 days ([Meta, Messaging limits](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/messaging-limits)).
- Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform §4.7 (last modified 23 September 2026): providers of "large language models, generative artificial intelligence platforms, general-purpose artificial intelligence assistants" are "strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Platform" when such technology is the "primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality". Platform data may not be used to train AI models. Under §5.3, Meta may "immediately suspend or terminate" access for breaches ([Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)).

### Inferences
- WhatsApp can only be a **second-touch** channel. Capture opt-in that names SolutionAI on a website form, email reply, event sign-up or click-to-WhatsApp ad (where the user messages first), log it, and only then send template messages.
- A WhatsApp bot that qualifies leads for SolutionAI's own services is "incidental" AI. Marketing SolutionAI's general AI assistant through WhatsApp, or letting MSME clients run general-purpose assistants on it, could breach §4.7. This needs product and legal review.
- Bulk cold sends would quickly cause blocks, low quality and tier freezes, putting the business portfolio at risk. Losing the account would cut off customer-service messaging as well.

### Gaps
- I did not verify India-specific measures, such as per-user marketing frequency caps or India pricing changes in 2025–2026. The developer pages I fetched did not describe quality-rating downgrade mechanics in detail.

## Q6. LinkedIn: User Agreement, hiQ v. LinkedIn, Proxycurl, and scraping LinkedIn from India

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's contract bans scraping, bots, and using LinkedIn data obtained through aggregators or brokers, and it is enforcing those terms successfully. hiQ ended in a 2022 consent judgment with a $500k judgment, a permanent injunction and data deletion. Proxycurl shut down in July 2025 after a January 2025 suit and was permanently enjoined. For non-EU users the terms choose California law and Santa Clara courts. No Indian court has ruled on LinkedIn scraping, but IT Act s.43 and s.66 and contract claims are available. Do not scrape LinkedIn or buy LinkedIn-derived data.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025):
  - §8.2(2) bans software, "robots… to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data".
  - §8.2(13) bans "bots or other unauthorized automated methods", including automated adding or downloading of contacts and sending messages.
  - §8.2(4) bans copying or using information "obtained from the Services", including through "search tools or data aggregators or brokers".
  - §8.2(11) bans monetising "related data".
  - §8.1(1) requires compliance with "anti-spam laws".
  - §6 makes California law govern, with courts in Santa Clara County, for users outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland.

  ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement))
- hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn: the Ninth Circuit affirmed hiQ's preliminary injunction on 9 September 2019. The Supreme Court vacated and remanded in light of *Van Buren* on 14 June 2021, and the Ninth Circuit reaffirmed in April 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)).
- On 4 November 2022 the district court held that hiQ breached the User Agreement by scraping and by creating fake accounts, although hiQ won on CFAA "unauthorized access" to public data. On 6 December 2022 the parties filed a stipulated consent judgment. Its terms:
  - a $500,000 judgment against hiQ;
  - a permanent injunction against scraping, fake accounts and building commercial services on LinkedIn data;
  - deletion of the code, algorithms and member data derived from scraping.

  ([National Law Review, 8 Dec 2022](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-landmark-scraping-case))
- Proxycurl: LinkedIn sued in January 2025. Proxycurl announced on 4 July 2025, "Today, we are shutting Proxycurl down", citing the need "to comply with the legal settlement with LinkedIn" and the cost of litigation ([Nubela blog, 4 Jul 2025](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)). A permanent injunction requires Proxycurl to delete all LinkedIn data obtained by unauthorised means and to notify its customers ([Social Media Today, 28 Jul 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)). One vendor blog gives 2026 dates, which conflicts with the primary post ([Bright Data](https://brightdata.com/blog/web-data/proxycurl-alternatives.md)). I treat 2025 as correct.
- In India the government has said scraping may breach IT Act s.43, and courts have so far looked at scraping mainly through IP law ([Spice Route Legal, Aug 2025](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)). Indian courts "have yet to determine whether these contractual or technical restrictions [terms of use, robots.txt] are enforceable" ([same](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)).

### Inferences
- An Indian company scraping LinkedIn faces several exposures. Logged-in scraping with company or fake accounts is a clear breach of the agreement it accepted, enforceable in California and, by contract, potentially in India. It also risks IT Act s.43(a)/(b) compensation claims and, if done dishonestly, s.66 criminal exposure. Founder personal data from LinkedIn is arguably "made publicly available by the Data Principal" under DPDP, but that does not cure the contract or IT Act issues.
- Safer options:
  - LinkedIn's own sanctioned products, with manual, human-paced outreach inside LinkedIn's limits;
  - LinkedIn ads and lead-gen forms, which capture consent;
  - founders' own websites and registry data.

  Avoid third-party "LinkedIn data" vendors, since §8.2(4) targets data obtained "through… data aggregators or brokers".

### Gaps
- I did not fetch LinkedIn's API and partner-program terms (Sales Navigator, Marketing API) to confirm what automated uses are allowed. I also found no Indian enforcement action by LinkedIn.

## Q7. MCA data: using MCA21 master and director data for marketing

### Takeaway
MCA company and director data is public by statute, which supports the DPDP s.3(c)(ii)(B) exclusion for the fields the law makes public. The MCA has also moved to shield contact identifiers in some registers. I could not read the MCA21 terms of use, which were blocked. Use registry data for company discovery and director names; do not bulk-harvest identity documents or personal contact details from filings.

### Cited Findings
- Filed DIR-12 forms become public documents "inspected by any person by paying a nominal fee". DIR-2 includes PAN, passport, Aadhaar and utility-bill documents. In April 2022 the MCA amended the rules so that members' email IDs, PAN and addresses in the register of members cannot be inspected or copied ([Conventus Law, Feb 2024](https://conventuslaw.com/?p=112869)).
- The exclusion's legal-obligation limb covers data published by a person "under an obligation under any law… to make such personal data publicly available" ([DPDP Act s.3(c)(ii)(B)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- Corporate filings that a law requires to be published fall within the exclusion's logic, but companies should still check website terms and laws on unauthorised access ([India Briefing, Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).

### Inferences
- Use MCA data (CIN, company name, incorporation date, registered address, company email, director names and DINs) as the discovery spine. Prefer official or licensed channels (MCA bulk data products or licensed resellers) over scraping MCA21 portal screens behind CAPTCHA. Defeating CAPTCHAs or rate limits looks like "access without permission" under IT Act s.43.
- Do not take identity attachments (PAN, Aadhaar, passport) from filings. That is outside any marketing purpose, and Aadhaar has its own statutory restrictions (not researched here).

### Gaps
- The MCA21 terms of use, any MCA or ROC action against data resellers or marketing misuse, and documented public complaints about post-incorporation spam (e.g. vendors calling newly incorporated founders) all remain unverified. ## Q8. Web scraping in India generally: IT Act s.43 and s.66, copyright, contract and terms of service

### Takeaway
No Indian statute names "scraping". Liability comes from IT Act s.43 (civil compensation for accessing or downloading data "without permission"), s.66 (criminal liability if dishonest or fraudulent), copyright in website content and compiled databases, and contract (terms of use). Indian courts have not settled whether terms-of-use or robots.txt restrictions bind scrapers.

### Cited Findings
- IT Act s.43: if any person "without permission of the owner… (a) accesses or secures access to such computer… [or] (b) downloads, copies or extracts any data, computer data base or information", they are "liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the person so affected". Section 66: doing a s.43 act "dishonestly or fraudulently" is punishable with imprisonment up to three years, a fine up to ₹5 lakh, or both ([IT Act 2000, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13116/1/it_act_2000_updated.pdf)).
- "The law in India neither explicitly defines the term data scraping nor regulates it". Courts have examined it "mainly in the context of intellectual property rights", and the government said in Parliament that scraping public data may breach s.43 ([Spice Route Legal, 1 Aug 2025](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)).
- The IAPP commentator calls reliance on s.43 "too broad" because it targets unauthorised access, not open sites. The minister also cited possible s.66 criminal liability ([IAPP, Oct 2025](https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-)).
- Lawful collection is a separate question from the DPDP exclusion. "Information obtained through unauthorised access or by circumventing technical or contractual restrictions raises separate legal questions" ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).

### Inferences
- Lower-risk scraping posture: public pages only, no login walls, honour robots.txt and terms of use, no CAPTCHA or rate-limit circumvention, polite crawl rates, extract facts rather than copying expressive content, and keep provenance logs. Use official APIs and bulk datasets where they exist (MCA, Startup India, state portals) and partnership feeds from incubators.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve specific Indian database-copyright cases (for example, *Eastern Book Co. v. D.B. Modak* (SC 2008) on originality, or Delhi HC cases on compiled customer databases) or the status of *ANI v. OpenAI* (Delhi HC). law.asia refers to a first ruling in that case but I did not read it. I found no Indian decision directly on scraping websites for B2B lead data.

## Q9. AI-specific rules: MeitY advisories, AI disclosure on calls, deepfake and voice rules (with the US FCC comparison)

### Takeaway
India has **no binding standalone AI law**, and I found **no Indian rule requiring callers to disclose an AI voice**. The relevant Indian levers are TRAI's robocall pre-declaration (Q4), the February 2026 IT Rules amendment on synthetically generated information (aimed at intermediaries' labelling and takedown duties), and general fraud and impersonation law. The US FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA and need prior express consent. That is a useful benchmark for designing AI calls.

### Cited Findings
- FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (adopted 2 February 2024, released 8 February 2024): "we confirm that the TCPA's restrictions on the use of 'artificial or prerecorded voice' encompass current AI technologies that generate human voices", which therefore "require the prior express consent of the called party". "Voice cloning" falls within the existing prohibition ([FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)).
- India amended the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021 by G.S.R. 120(E) on 10 February 2026, effective 20 February 2026. The amendment adds rules on synthetic or AI-generated content, including labelling (with embedded metadata where possible) and takedown handling, and intermediaries that fail to comply risk losing safe harbour. The same source says India still has no binding standalone AI law ([Wikipedia, Regulation of AI, India section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence)). This is a secondary source and should be checked against the Gazette.
- TRAI requires senders to pre-declare auto-dialer or robo-call use and its objective in writing, and operators must detect "robotic calls, auto dialer calls or pre-recorded announcements" ([TRAI Regulation 2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)).
- Meta's WhatsApp terms bar general-purpose AI assistants as primary functionality ([Meta Terms §4.7](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)).

### Inferences
- Design the Hindi and English AI voice agent to:
  - say at the start that it is an AI assistant calling on behalf of SolutionAI, with a human hand-off option;
  - never clone a real person's voice;
  - call only consented numbers via the 140-series with pre-declaration;
  - record consent and calls (with notice) for audit.

  This satisfies the strictest benchmark (FCC) and avoids any "deceive the recipient" characterisation, which TRAI now classes as UCC.
- If SolutionAI generates and distributes synthetic audio or video (for example, AI founder videos) through platforms, the platforms' new labelling duties will flow down. Label SolutionAI's AI-generated media proactively.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the text of MeitY's March 2024 AI advisories or the India AI Governance Guidelines (reported as November 2025). I also could not check the primary Gazette text of G.S.R. 120(E), since the search budget was exhausted and MeitY's site blocks automated fetches. I found no Indian enforcement action involving AI voice telemarketing.

## Q10. Practical compliance architecture for the lead engine

### Takeaway
Build around five controls:
1. a **per-field provenance and lawful-basis register**;
2. a **consent ledger** that mirrors DPDP Rule 3, TRAI DCA and WhatsApp opt-in requirements;
3. a **global suppression list** checked before every channel action;
4. **channel gating**: email first, with telecom and WhatsApp only after consent;
5. **logs, retention and erasure** aligned to DPDP Rules 6 and 8.

### Cited Findings (requirements the architecture must meet)
- Under DPDP, the notice must itemise the data and purposes and give withdrawal, rights and complaint routes. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. Consent must be limited to necessary data ([DPDP Rules r.3](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); [DPDP Act s.6](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- Also under DPDP:
  - The fiduciary must prove notice and consent if challenged (s.6(10)).
  - Erasure on withdrawal must extend to processors (s.8(7)).
  - Security minimums and one-year log retention apply (r.6).
  - Personal data and processing logs must be kept for at least one year (r.8(3)).
  - Breaches go to the Board within 72 hours (r.7).
  - Processor contracts are required (s.8(2), r.6(1)(f)).

  ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf))
- TRAI requires:
  - DLT registration of sender, headers and templates;
  - an opt-out in each promotional message;
  - no re-request for consent within 90 days of an opt-out;
  - 7-day validity for transaction consent;
  - DCA (OTP-verified) consent to get past DND preferences;
  - robocall pre-declaration;
  - 140-series for promotional calls.

  ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf); [TRAI Regulation](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf))
- WhatsApp requires that the number was given by the person, an opt-in naming the business, templates for business-initiated messages, and honouring opt-outs made "on or off WhatsApp" ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).
- The email benchmark is to honour opt-outs within 10 business days and include sender identity and a postal address ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).
- Record how each data item became public and the basis for relying on s.3(c)(ii), and run due diligence on purchased datasets ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)).

### Inferences (recommended design)
1. **Data model.** Separate the **Company entity** (MCA, DPIIT, portals: mostly non-personal) from the **Person entity** (founder or director). Each Person field (name, role, email, phone, LinkedIn URL) carries these attributes:
   - source and source type: registry under legal obligation, self-published, third-party published, or licensed vendor;
   - capture timestamp and capture method (API, bulk data, crawl);
   - lawful-basis tag: excluded under 3(c)(ii)(A) or (B), consent, or none.
   
   Fields tagged "none" may not be used for outreach. Do not ingest LinkedIn-derived or broker-sourced personal mobiles.
2. **Consent ledger.** Store append-only events: notice version shown (hash), purposes, channels (email, WhatsApp, SMS, voice), language, timestamp, capture surface, proof (OTP or DCA reference, form payload, WhatsApp opt-in text), and withdrawal events. Notices must be available in English and Hindi (s.5(3)). Prepare to interoperate with registered Consent Managers from November 2026.
3. **Suppression service.** Use one global do-not-contact list keyed on normalised email, E.164 phone and hashed identifiers, checked synchronously before every send or dial on every channel. Opt-out on any channel suppresses all marketing channels. Enforce the TRAI 90-day no-re-ask window. Keep the suppression entry when erasing the profile; it serves a legal-obligation purpose and holds minimal data.
4. **Channel gating.**
   - *Email:* the cold first touch is allowed only to role inboxes or s.3(c)(ii)-tagged addresses, with sender identity, address and one-click unsubscribe.
   - *WhatsApp:* only after an explicit opt-in naming SolutionAI.
   - *SMS:* DLT-registered -P header and template, with opt-out in each message.
   - *AI voice:* 140-series via a registered telemarketer, pre-declared to the operator, DND-scrubbed or DCA-consented, with an AI disclosure at the start, human transfer, and recorded consent to call.
   - *Rate limits and quality monitors:* auto-pause campaigns on complaint or block spikes. TRAI acts at 5 complaints in 10 days; WhatsApp limits on low quality.
5. **Minimisation and retention.** Collect only what the outreach purpose needs. Auto-expire unconverted, non-consented Person records (for example, 6–12 months). Keep processing logs for at least one year (r.6, r.8(3)), then delete.
6. **Vendors.** Use DPDP s.8(2) contracts with enrichment, CPaaS, telephony, LLM and WhatsApp BSP vendors, covering security, sub-processing, deletion on instruction and breach notice within hours. Include TRAI PE–TM traceability clauses in telemarketer contracts. LLM vendors may not train on WhatsApp data (Meta §4.7).
7. **Audit and rights.** Keep immutable audit logs of data access and outreach decisions. Run a rights-request workflow with a 90-day SLA (aim much faster) and a published grievance and DPO contact (r.9). Prepare a 72-hour breach runbook.
8. **Governance timeline.**
   - Now: TRAI, WhatsApp, LinkedIn and IT Act compliance.
   - By 13 Nov 2026: consent-manager readiness.
   - By 13 May 2027: full DPDP compliance (notices, rights, security, retention).
   
   Watch for any MeitY move to compress timelines, Board appointments, and DoT rules under Telecom Act s.28.

### Gaps
- I found no Indian regulator guidance on a "consent ledger" format. DCA integration specifics (APIs, which operators expose DCA to which senders) were not verified.
- An Indian counsel review is recommended on: the role split between SolutionAI and its clients (fiduciary vs processor), the scope of s.3(c)(ii) for incubator and state-portal data, and the WhatsApp §4.7 AI-provider risk.
